Why this matters

Cyber attacks often begin not with code, but with a person making a small decision at the wrong moment.

Cyber attacks usually begin with people

When a major cyber attack appears on the news, it is easy to imagine expert hackers breaking through sophisticated computer systems. In reality, many attacks begin by exploiting people through phishing, password reuse, and social engineering.

One mistake can spread surprisingly far

Reusing a password or trusting the wrong email can affect your bank account, workplace, friends, and personal information. Small decisions often have much larger consequences.

Understanding attacks is the best defence

CyberTrail does not just tell you what happened—it lets you experience how attacks unfold, investigate the evidence, and understand how they could have been prevented.

Cyber Toolkit

Learning doesn't stop when a scenario ends. After each investigation, CyberTrail recommends practical tools that help you apply the security habits you've just learned. You can also explore them independently at any time. They help you test password strength, generate safer passwords, and scan messages for scam warning signs.

Password Strength Meter

Estimate how hard a password is to guess, and see why common words, repeated patterns, and leaked passwords are risky even when they look complicated.

Password Generator

Create random passwords or memorable passphrases using secure randomness, not predictable shortcuts like Math.random().

Scam Message Scanner

Paste in an email or text and check it for urgency, suspicious links, pressure tactics, and other common phishing patterns.
